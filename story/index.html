Chapter 1 · The eight-hour wall

Almost one pair in four, never

of pairs, almost one pair in four, never share a working hour in the whole year. Not a single row of the table where both are at work.

It is a wall, not a slope. For pairs whose gap holds all year, under 7½ hours apart every one shares an hour; 8 or more apart, not one does. A working day is eight hours long, so at eight hours apart one person’s day ends just as the other’s begins.

At exactly 7½ hours the half hour they share is not a whole row, so it depends on whose hours you count: from Fortaleza, Kabul shares an hour; from Kabul, Fortaleza does not.

The clocks, not the calendar

Chapter 3 · Awake, though

Someone takes the call in the evening

The waking day is longer. Count 7 am to 11 pm, and the share of pairs who are never awake at the same time is : every pair has hours when both are up. The pairs who never meet at work are both awake for a day, in the middle. The call happens; one of them takes it before work or after dinner.

Chapter 4 · The wall moves

London and Los Angeles, for twenty days

Clocks change, on different dates or not at all. For of pairs, about one in three, the gap is not the same all year, and for that moves them across the wall.

London and Los Angeles are eight hours apart, so they never meet, except in the weeks when America has changed its clocks and Britain has not yet: then they are seven apart, and share an hour on 20 working days of the year. London and Shanghai share one only while Britain is on summer time.

Epilogue · Reading When Works?

Look for the eight

On When Works?, two towns eight hours apart or more never have a row where both are green, on any date. The clock changes it lists underneath are where that can change.
